# Ten directions / 60 questions (V2.3)

## Common working principles
1. Identify evident opportunities and material risks in a small, manually selected set, not a comprehensive audit. Judge broad cheapness/expensiveness and realistic major-loss risk, not a precise target or loss probability. A clear final conclusion does not justify skipping investigation.
2. Research the assigned direction using the preceding 3–5 years and latest available information as of the cutoff. Reuse reliable materials/calculations without repeating the whole company. Price-blind stages judge the business; final synthesis judges the investment. The historical window does not change the future horizon.
3. Focus on economic substance. Use qualitative judgment when sufficient and simple supported estimates where needed. Refine only if greater precision could change the decision; disclosed facts and basic bases still require accuracy.
4. Treat gaps by materiality. Explain mechanism and, where defensible, rough scale relative to the appropriate group denominator, period and ownership. Exposure is not loss magnitude or probability. Missing information is not itself evidence of a major risk. Stop on immaterial differences; pursue potentially decisive unbounded issues.
5. Give the best-supported judgment before limitations. Mark assumptions; do not demand certainty about all future events. Local unknowns limit local claims; the synthesizer independently assesses group consequences. Small assumption changes that reverse cheapness prevent a claim of obvious undervaluation. No evident bargain does not make the business unassessable.
6. Keep originals, currency, units, periods, cash rights and duplication clear. Company cash generation, distributable company cash and investor receipts differ. Use supplied strategy definitions; missing parameters are an interface limitation, not a business risk. Use actual calculation tools and never present arithmetic as realized future facts.
7. Give each typed conclusion and 2–4 sentences of decisive reasons, evidence and conditions. Explain what obtainable evidence/estimate would end a wait. Do not repeat background, logs or disclaimers, write another long report per question, or decide by score/pass count.

Research thoroughly and judge approximately with evidence. Tolerate immaterial error without inventing certainty or avoiding judgment. Read one direction at a time. “Unresolved / not applicable” are also allowed for every question, with reasons. Preserve IDs for a full review; targeted work answers only its assigned subset.

## 01 Earnings foundation and demand

Identify how the business earns and whether it is more likely stable, improving or declining. Exact profit attribution for every business is unnecessary; use scale, group trends and mechanism. Stability need not depend on high growth.

- **01.Q1** Are the main profit sources reasonably clear? (yes / no / conditional)
- **01.Q2** Is core demand more likely to improve, remain stable or deteriorate? (improving / stable / deteriorating)
- **01.Q3** Are recent changes more likely sustained deterioration than short-term fluctuation? (yes / no / conditional)
- **01.Q4** When the core business is under pressure, are net offsets from other businesses or operating adjustments roughly sufficient? (yes / no / conditional)
- **01.Q5** How material are these changes to sustained group cash generation? (none / small / material / severe)
- **01.Q6** Can existing earnings and cash capacity broadly be maintained? (yes / no / conditional)

## 02 Competitive advantage and durability

Identify what protects earnings and whether it is weakening. No wide moat is not automatic rejection. Where ROIC is missing, use comparable operating performance and competitive changes rather than filling indicators.

- **02.Q1** Do actual competitive advantages support current profitability? (yes / no / conditional)
- **02.Q2** Are core advantages improving, stable or deteriorating? (improving / stable / deteriorating)
- **02.Q3** How large are competitive, substitution and price-war threats? (low / medium / high)
- **02.Q4** Does the additional investment needed to maintain competitiveness materially erode cash? (yes / no / conditional)
- **02.Q5** Do recent earnings, cash and investment patterns support durable advantages? (yes / no / conditional)
- **02.Q6** Can the main earnings foundation survive reasonably adverse conditions? (yes / no / conditional)

## 03 Earnings quality and shareholder cash

Test conversion of profit to real cash and broad operating support for returns. Investigate major items first; do not demand full reconciliation of immaterial deposits/settlements, but retain material ownership, restrictions and obligations.

- **03.Q1** Is reported profit supported by operating cash? (yes / no / conditional)
- **03.Q2** Have the main necessary expenditures been considered? (yes / no / conditional)
- **03.Q3** Is cash unavailable to or not attributable to ordinary shareholders material? (yes / no / conditional)
- **03.Q4** Are there obvious omissions, duplicate deductions or basis errors that affect the conclusion? (yes / no / conditional)
- **03.Q5** Is the current ordinary-dividend scale broadly sustainable? (yes / no / conditional)
- **03.Q6** How materially do required cash-basis corrections affect the investment premise? (none / small / material / severe)

## 04 Balance sheet and survival pressure

Assess realistic solvency/refinancing problems and shareholder harm. Separate baseline from tighter-financing stress. Neither guarantee normal refinancing nor assume all financing stops. A daily cash schedule is not required for conditional resilience judgments.

- **04.Q1** Are available funds and major rigid obligations of comparable magnitude? (yes / no / conditional)
- **04.Q2** How concentrated are maturities and how strong is refinancing dependence? (low / medium / high)
- **04.Q3** Can the business broadly maintain normal operations under reasonable operating stress? (yes / no / conditional)
- **04.Q4** Do upstreaming restrictions, guarantees or creditor priority create material risk? (yes / no / conditional)
- **04.Q5** How much damage could forced financing or asset sales cause? (none / small / material / severe)
- **04.Q6** Is the overall financial buffer sufficient for the main risks? (yes / no / conditional)

## 05 Capital allocation and investment discipline

Assess major failed investments and whether they still consume value. Focus on material spending and continuing conduct rather than auditing every past project. Missing project IRRs do not preclude judgment. Separate absorbed historic losses from future losses.

- **05.Q1** Are recent major uses of funds consistent with shareholder interests? (yes / no / conditional)
- **05.Q2** How damaging are identified major investment failures? (none / small / material / severe)
- **05.Q3** Have these failed projects broadly stopped consuming further funds? (yes / no / conditional)
- **05.Q4** How materially will major future spending commitments squeeze shareholder cash? (none / small / material / severe)
- **05.Q5** How high is the risk from expansion, acquisitions or unrelated diversification? (low / medium / high)
- **05.Q6** Is future capital-use discipline broadly trustworthy? (yes / no / conditional)

## 06 Governance, incentives and delivery of returns

Assess trustworthiness and fair shareholder participation. Distinguish checked major records from individual unchecked promises. Do not exhaust every commitment or demand private contracts. No payment confirmation does not prove payment or default.

- **06.Q1** Have material integrity problems been found that remain substantively unresolved? (yes / no / conditional)
- **06.Q2** How strongly do incentives conflict with existing shareholders’ interests? (low / medium / high)
- **06.Q3** Have the major return commitments actually checked broadly been delivered? (yes / no / conditional)
- **06.Q4** How materially do net dilution and buyback purposes affect per-share rights? (none / small / material / severe)
- **06.Q5** Do major related-party arrangements create a realistic risk of harm to ordinary shareholders? (yes / no / conditional)
- **06.Q6** Is the premise that ordinary shareholders can fairly receive future returns broadly established? (yes / no / conditional)

## 07A Independent valuation and margin of safety: before quotes

Before quotes, choose one suitable simple method and supported baseline/adverse ranges with key sensitivity. Future assumptions need not already be proved in accounts. Six-year company cash coverage is separate from intrinsic value. A case cheap only through precise tuning is not clearly undervalued.

- **07.Q1** Does the valuation basis reflect the business’s economic substance? (yes / no / conditional)
- **07.Q2** Can a baseline value range be broadly established? (yes / no / conditional)
- **07.Q3** How materially do reasonably adverse scenarios affect value? (none / small / material / severe)
- **07.Q4** How sensitive is the conclusion to key assumptions? (low / medium / high)

## 07B Independent valuation and margin of safety: after quotes

Unlock the task quote and apply 07A’s existing assumptions and actual arithmetic. Supported ranges suffice. Explain and recalculate factual corrections without tuning to price. Reconcile disagreement with 09 on a common economic basis.

- **07.Q5** Is the current price clearly undervalued, moderately undervalued, fair or expensive? (clearly undervalued / moderately undervalued / fair / expensive)
- **07.Q6** Is there still an evident margin of safety under reasonably adverse conditions? (yes / no / conditional)

## 08 Broad-market and industry valuation

Assess broad valuation and environmental risk. Industry labels only identify candidates: match actual business models and earnings bases, comparing different businesses separately. Representative peers may support a bounded view, not one stock as the whole industry. Loss/abnormal PE is not cheapness; use suitable alternatives and operating evidence. No exact percentile or full-universe claim is required. A separately authorized ten-year window is sensitivity only.

- **08.Q1** What is the broad market’s relative valuation position? (clearly undervalued / moderately undervalued / fair / expensive)
- **08.Q2** What is the valuation position of the industry or a representative peer sample? (clearly undervalued / moderately undervalued / fair / expensive)
- **08.Q3** How fragile are credit, financing conditions and investment sentiment? (low / medium / high)
- **08.Q4** Are valuation signals materially distorted by abnormal earnings or sample changes? (yes / no / conditional)
- **08.Q5** What are the main effects of a market downturn on this transaction, and how material are they? (none / small / material / severe)
- **08.Q6** Does the current environment offer additional valuation support? (yes / no / conditional)

## 09 Transaction returns and realization conditions

Evaluate return appeal and dependence at the reference price under explicit funding assumptions, before a trade occurs. Missing personal position data limits suitability. Dividends can be reasoned estimates but not invented promises. Separate company cash coverage from investor after-tax receipts.

- **09.Q1** Are the reference purchase price and funding assumptions sufficient for conditional analysis? (yes / no / conditional)
- **09.Q2** Are there reasonable holding-return sources without valuation rerating? (yes / no / conditional)
- **09.Q3** How dependent are returns on growth, catalysts or optimistic realization? (low / medium / high)
- **09.Q4** How material are the main effects of tax, fees, FX, liquidity and waiting costs? (none / small / material / severe)
- **09.Q5** Is risk/reward attractive relative to reasonably available alternatives? (yes / no / conditional)
- **09.Q6** Does the current price merit entering a human investment decision? (yes / no / conditional)

## 10 Independent countercase and material loss

Actively test the most realistic material-loss path and its counterevidence. Unproven safety is not proof of major risk; do not manufacture fatal issues. Withdraw weakened objections. Separate temporary price falls, dividend adjustments and permanent value destruction.

- **10.Q1** How likely, in broad terms, is the best-supported path to material loss? (low / medium / high)
- **10.Q2** If it occurs, how severe is the damage to shareholder cash or equity? (none / small / material / severe)
- **10.Q3** Could several risks breach the same protection at once? (yes / no / conditional)
- **10.Q4** Is the low price more likely to reflect continuing destruction of value? (yes / no / conditional)
- **10.Q5** Does the strongest opposing case withstand contrary evidence? (yes / no / conditional)
- **10.Q6** Is there a decisive issue sufficient to reject or defer this transaction? (yes / no / conditional)
